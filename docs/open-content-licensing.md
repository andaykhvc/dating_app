# Tatoeba, Wikidata and CLDR clear the commercial bar

Three sources can be imported into our Supabase database and served to free and paying users with no share-alike obligation: **Tatoeba sentence text (CC BY 2.0 FR, with a CC0 subset), Wikidata Lexemes (CC0 1.0) and Unicode CLDR as exposed through ICU/`Intl` (Unicode License V3)**. Together they should form the core. Around them sits a group of attribution-only extras that are also safe: Open English WordNet (CC BY 4.0), the Spanish Multilingual Central Repository wordnet (CC BY 3.0) and the CEFR-J English wordlist (free for commercial use with citation). The share-alike sources need more care. Wiktionary/kaikki, Open Dutch WordNet, OMW's Wiktionary-derived German and Turkish files, and FrequencyWords all allow commercial use, but CC BY-SA 4.0 turns our database compilation into "Adapted Material" once we import a substantial portion of theirs. They belong in quarantined tables or serve only as internal ranking signals. We should avoid four things outright. LibreLingo's Spanish course carries a NonCommercial LICENSE file. OpenSubtitles text is unowned by its distributors. Tatoeba audio is licensed per speaker and often non-commercial. The Goethe, Cervantes and Cambridge level lists are proprietary. None of this is legal advice. The Tatoeba and CLDR terms were verified from primary source files, but the Wikidata, Wiktionary, OPUS and Leipzig terms came through search snippets because the research sandbox blocked those sites, so a lawyer's pass is prudent before launch.

## Five licenses separate "ship it" from "keep it internal"

## The permissive core: sentences, lexemes and locale data

### Tatoeba supplies sentence pairs under attribution-only terms

Every Tatoeba sentence carries its own license. Tatoeba's code defines exactly three sentence-license values: **`CC BY 2.0 FR` (the default), `CC0 1.0`, and an empty "Licensing issue" value**, which is excluded from exports ([Licenses.php](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/src/Lib/Licenses.php)). No sentence can therefore be NC, ND or SA. The Terms of Use say CC BY imposes "a single restriction on the use, reuse, modification and distribution of the sentence: a condition of attribution". They also say Tatoeba is "not generally opposed to using our content for commercial purposes". Their commercial and no-derivatives caveats target audio ([Terms of Use §6.2, §6.5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/resources/locales/en/terms-of-use.html)). Commercial use, normalizing punctuation, storing sentences in Postgres and serving them behind a paywall are all permitted, as long as attribution follows the text.

The exports hide license at the row level. `sentences.csv` and `sentences_detailed.csv` include both CC BY and CC0 sentences but **have no license column**. The CC0 subset ships separately as `sentences_CC0` ([weekly_exports.sql](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/docs/database/scripts/weekly_exports.sql)). The importer should left-join `sentences_CC0` on ID and store `license = 'CC0 1.0'` where it matches, and `'CC BY 2.0 FR'` otherwise. It should recompute that value on every import, because admins may correct a license after abuse. The ToU permit changes only toward more restrictive licenses, and a CC BY sentence may never be re-added as CC0 ([Terms of Use §6.4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/resources/locales/en/terms-of-use.html)).

Two sources set different bars for attribution. The ToU wording is strict: "in the case of a CC-BY license, it is your responsibility to quote the author of the sentence" ([Terms of Use §6.5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/resources/locales/en/terms-of-use.html)). The wiki is looser: "write somewhere that some/all of your sentences are from Tatoeba, with a link to https://tatoeba.org, and mention that Tatoeba's data is released under CC-BY 2.0 FR" ([Using the Tatoeba Corpus](https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/articles/show/using-the-tatoeba-corpus)). We should meet the stricter bar. Each sentence gets a tap-to-reveal credit reading **"Tatoeba sentence #ID by USERNAME — CC BY 2.0 FR"**, linking to `https://tatoeba.org/en/sentences/show/{id}` and to the license deed. A global credits page adds that some sentences are CC0 and that sentences may have been lightly edited. The only author field in the exports is `username`, and it is `\N` for orphan sentences ([weekly_exports.sql](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/docs/database/scripts/weekly_exports.sql)). Those fall back to "Tatoeba contributors". The translation links carry no author and no separate license, and the Downloads page places all export files under CC BY 2.0 FR ([downloads.php](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/templates/Pages/downloads.php)). The global credit covers them.

Content quality matters more than usual for a dating-adjacent product. Tatoeba states that translation accuracy is "not guaranteed by any professional intervention". It also allows offensive sentences, because "the reality of languages is also composed of popular, slang and vulgar registers" ([Terms of Use §5.1, §7](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/resources/locales/en/terms-of-use.html)). The community also acknowledges an overload of Tom-and-Mary sentences ([Wall #32296](https://tatoeba.org/en/wall/show_message/32296)). A profanity filter is therefore mandatory. The exports offer three ranking signals: the experimental `users_sentences.csv` reviews (−1, 0 or 1), `sentences_base` for preferring native originals over translations, and `user_languages` for preferring native-speaker owners ([downloads.php](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/templates/Pages/downloads.php)).

### Wikidata Lexemes give CC0 grammar, with one contamination risk

"All structured data in the main, property and lexeme namespaces is made available under the Creative Commons CC0 License" ([Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing)). The waiver extends to EntitySchemas ([Wikidata:Copyright](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Copyright)). Attribution is optional, modification and relicensing are unrestricted, and CC0 also waives EU sui generis database rights as far as the law allows. A courtesy line such as "Lexical data from Wikidata (CC0)" costs nothing. The residual risk comes from contributors, not the license. Community policy requires that sense definitions "originate from public domain dictionaries" ([Lexicographical data/Documentation](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Lexicographical_data/Documentation)). CC0 cannot launder text that was copied in breach of that rule. **We should import lemmas, forms, lexical category, grammatical gender (P5185) and cross-language sense links**, which are facts or too short to be copyrightable. We should write our own glosses and not display usage examples (P5831) verbatim.

For concept-to-word mapping, P5137 "item for this sense" points each sense at a language-neutral Q-item. P5972 "translation" links senses directly ([Documentation/Senses](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Lexicographical_data/Documentation/Senses)). Mapping concept → Q-item → senses → lexemes filtered by language yields Hund / perro / hond / köpek / dog with genders attached. Coverage is the open question. A 2020 survey did not list German, Spanish, Dutch or Turkish among the top lexeme languages ([Nielsen, LDL-2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.ldl-1.12.pdf)). Current counts are tracked on a [statistics page](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Lexicographical_data/Statistics/Count_of_lexemes,_forms,_and_senses_by_language) that the research could not read. Expect manual gap-filling for an A1 core of roughly 500–1,500 words, especially where lexemes lack senses.

### CLDR data costs one license notice

CLDR data (apart from the LDML specification text) and ICU are both under **Unicode License V3**, an MIT-style license that grants the right "to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, and/or sell copies". Its one condition is that "this copyright and permission notice appear with all copies ... or ... in associated Documentation" ([CLDR LICENSE](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr/blob/main/LICENSE); [ICU LICENSE](https://github.com/unicode-org/icu/blob/main/LICENSE)). The research ran Node v22.22.2 (ICU 78.2, CLDR 48.0) and got complete weekday and month names, language and region names, currency names and relative-time words ("mañana") for all five locales. For example, Turkish gives Pazartesi…Pazar and Ocak, Şubat, Mart, and German gives "Türkei". Browsers and Node ship Unicode notices for their own ICU copies ([Node LICENSE](https://github.com/nodejs/node/blob/main/LICENSE)). Once we extract full tables into our database and serve them, however, we are redistributing CLDR Data Files ourselves. The conservative move is to **paste the full Unicode License V3 text into the app's open-source notices page**.

Two practical wrinkles follow. First, CLDR strings change between releases: English now renders TR as "Türkiye" rather than "Turkey". Store the CLDR version with each row. Second, `Intl` has **no number spellout**. `notation: "spellout"` throws a RangeError, and the ECMA-402 proposal for it remains open ([tc39/ecma402#494](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/issues/494)). Spelled-out numbers must be precomputed from CLDR's RBNF rules in `common/rbnf/{de,es,nl,tr,en}.xml`, which include German case- and gender-inflected cardinals and ordinals ([CLDR rbnf](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr/tree/main/common/rbnf)). That output falls under the same notice. CLDR does not supply noun gender, demonyms or example sentences, and Wikidata fills the first of those gaps.

## Attribution-only extras fill sense and level gaps

Wordnets add synonym and sense structure but little A1 pedagogy. The Open Multilingual Wordnet notes that all its wordnets "permit redistribution", but licenses vary ([omw-data index.toml](https://github.com/omwn/omw-data/blob/main/index.toml)). The **Spanish MCR wordnet is CC BY 3.0** (78,948 synsets, 166,281 lemma rows), with its English synset data under the original WordNet license ([wns/mcr/LICENSE](https://github.com/omwn/omw-data/blob/main/wns/mcr/LICENSE)). **Open English WordNet is CC BY 4.0**, and its latest release is the 2025 edition ([globalwordnet/english-wordnet](https://github.com/globalwordnet/english-wordnet)). Both allow commercial storage and redistribution with a credit and license link, plus a modification note for CC BY 3.0. The gap is structural. **OMW has no curated German or Turkish wordnet.** Those two languages appear only in the auto-extracted Wiktionary layer (CC BY-SA) and the CLDR layer (permissive) ([omw-data README](https://github.com/omwn/omw-data/blob/main/README.md)). OdeNet and Turkish wordnets outside OMW were not checked.

For English level tagging, the **CEFR-J Wordlist v1.5** "can be used for research and commercial purposes with no charge, provided that you cite the dataset properly", with copyright held by Tono Laboratory at TUFS ([olp-en-cefrj](https://github.com/openlanguageprofiles/olp-en-cefrj)). The same repo bundles the Octanove C1/C2 list, which is CC BY-SA 4.0 and outside our A1–A2 scope, so leave it out. The Leipzig Corpora Collection describes its text as available "under the creative commons attribution license (CC BY)" across 250+ languages, including all five of ours ([Leipzig Corpora Collection](https://www.wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en)). If the download page confirms CC BY without an NC clause, Leipzig becomes the cleanest source of frequency data and extra example sentences. Until then it is promising but unverified.

## Share-alike sources reach into our database

The CC BY-SA 4.0 clause that matters is §4(b). If we include "all or a substantial portion of the database contents" in our own database, "the database in which You have Sui Generis Database Rights (but not its individual contents) is Adapted Material" ([CC BY-SA 4.0 text](https://github.com/omwn/omw-data/blob/main/wns/nld/LICENSE)). Bulk-importing Wiktionary translations into our main vocabulary table would therefore oblige us to license that compilation, and every Wiktionary-derived row, under CC BY-SA 4.0 when we share it. The CEFR-level curation we want to keep proprietary would come along with it.

**Wiktionary** is dual-licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 and GFDL ([Wiktionary:Copyrights](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Copyrights)). It moved from 3.0 in June 2023 ([CC blog](https://creativecommons.org/2023/06/29/wikipedia-moves-to-cc-4-0-licenses/)). kaikki.org's per-edition extracts (English, German, Dutch, Spanish and Turkish Wiktionaries) carry "the same licenses as Wiktionary" ([kaikki.org](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/index.html)), while the wiktextract extraction tool itself is MIT ([wiktextract](https://github.com/tatuylonen/wiktextract/blob/master/README.md)). Commercial use is fine. Attribution means "From Wiktionary, CC BY-SA 4.0", a link to the entry, and a modification note, which CC BY-SA 4.0 lets us provide via hyperlink. If we use it, it must live in separate tables with provenance columns and never be merged into proprietary rows. **Open Dutch WordNet** (CC BY-SA 4.0) and OMW's **German and Turkish Wiktionary layers** (CC BY-SA 3.0 + GFDL) need the same quarantine ([wns/wikt/LICENSE](https://github.com/omwn/omw-data/blob/main/wns/wikt/LICENSE)).

**FrequencyWords** declares "MIT License for code. CC-by-sa-4.0 for content." Its 2018 lists for de, en, es, nl and tr are built from OpenSubtitles2018 ([FrequencyWords README](https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords/blob/master/README.md)). A word's frequency is a fact. Using the ranks only to decide lesson order produces lessons that do not reproduce the list. Shipping a `frequency_rank` column copied for thousands of words, however, looks like extracting a substantial portion, and it inherits the subtitle-copyright doubts described below. **Use it as a private, blended ordering signal and never expose it.** The Kelly lists are also share-alike and lack German, Spanish, Dutch and Turkish ([Språkbanken Kelly](https://spraakbanken.gu.se/en/projects/kelly)), so they add little.

## Four sources fail on commercial rights or provenance

**LibreLingo** fails on both counts. Its software is AGPL-3.0 and the repository is archived: "I am currently not maintaining LibreLingo" ([README](https://github.com/LibreLingo/LibreLingo/blob/main/README.md)). The Spanish-from-English course's LICENSE file is the full **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** text and has been since its initial 2021 commit, yet its `course.yaml` claims CC BY-SA 4.0 ([ES-from-EN LICENSE](https://github.com/LibreLingo/LibreLingo-ES-from-EN/blob/main/LICENSE)). The conservative reading bars commercial use. The course holds only about 82 new words and 112 phrases. The German and Dutch courses live on Codeberg with unverified licenses, and no Turkish course or English-target course exists ([config.json](https://github.com/LibreLingo/LibreLingo/blob/main/apps/librelingo-web/src/courses/config.json)). The value is too small to justify the risk.

**OPUS OpenSubtitles** carries an ODC-BY label on the 2024 release. The same page says the dataset "redistributes subtitle texts originally contributed to OpenSubtitles.org", that the authors "do not claim ownership", and that a takedown policy applies ([HF OpenSubtitles2024](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Helsinki-NLP/OpenSubtitles2024)). OPUS itself points to "OpenSubtitles Terms of Use" ([OPUS](https://opus.nlpl.eu/)). An open label from a party that owns nothing cannot license film dialogue. Serving those sentence pairs in a paid app is the highest-risk option on this list, while aggregate statistics carry far less risk.

**Tatoeba audio** is licensed per speaker. The options run from CC0 and CC BY 4.0 through CC BY-NC 4.0 and CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 to none at all, and the Downloads page warns that an empty license means "you may not reuse the audio outside the Tatoeba project" ([Licenses.php](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/src/Lib/Licenses.php); [downloads.php](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/templates/Pages/downloads.php)). Speakers can change the setting at any time. Use our own TTS instead. If audio is ever wanted, allow-list only CC0 and CC BY 4.0 rows and re-check them on every import.

The **Goethe-Institut, Instituto Cervantes and Cambridge English Vocabulary Profile** lists are proprietary. The Cervantes Plan Curricular pages read "Reservados todos los derechos" ([CVC](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ensenanza/biblioteca_ele/plan_curricular/niveles/09_nociones_especificas_inventario_a1-a2.htm)), the English Vocabulary Profile reportedly disallows commercial use ([Wikipedia: English Profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Profile)), and no open license was found for the [Goethe A1 Wortliste](https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/de/A1_SD1_Wortliste_02.pdf). Editors may consult them. Nobody should copy them.

## Download files and usage rules for the pipeline

All three core sources publish bulk dumps, and bulk dumps are the intended route. The Tatoeba ToU forbid "automated use of our Website that can overload and slow down our services" ([Terms of Use §7](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tatoeba/tatoeba2/dev/resources/locales/en/terms-of-use.html)). The Wikidata Query Service enforces a 60-second query deadline, 5 parallel queries per IP, and 60 seconds of query time per minute per IP + User-Agent ([WDQS User Manual](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikidata_Query_Service/User_Manual)). It returns HTTP 403 to clients without a descriptive User-Agent containing contact details ([Wikimedia User-Agent Policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Wikimedia_Foundation_User-Agent_Policy)). A weekly offline import that mirrors files to our own storage meets both sets of rules. The app should never hotlink either service at runtime.

## Conclusion

The open ecosystem supplies raw material but not a curriculum. The core sources give us sentences, grammar and locale vocabulary. None of the permissive sources gives CEFR level assignments for German, Spanish, Dutch or Turkish, because the authoritative lists (Goethe, Cervantes) are proprietary and CEFR-J covers English only. Leveling is where our proprietary value will sit. Leveling is also exactly what a careless import of CC BY-SA data would force us to open up under §4(b). The architecture should follow from that: every imported row carries `source`, `source_id`, `license`, `author` and `source_version` columns, and share-alike rows live in a separate schema, so our curation stays clean by construction rather than by later audit.

Three open items should be resolved before launch. Tatoeba's CC BY 2.0 FR predates explicit database-right language, which leaves a small, untested EU question about bulk extraction that a lawyer can close quickly. Leipzig's exact license terms need checking, because a confirmed CC BY would let us replace FrequencyWords with legally clean frequencies for all five languages. And Wikidata's German, Dutch and Turkish sense coverage should be measured on the actual dump, since how thin it is decides how much of the A1 core we write by hand.
